# Write an evidence brief

Use to turn several notes, sources, or observations into a decision-focused summary.

1. State the question the brief must answer.
2. Group evidence by claim rather than by the order of sources.
3. Lead with the strongest supported finding. For each important claim, identify the source and distinguish observation, inference, and unresolved question.
4. Compare disagreements directly: do the sources refer to different dates, groups, methods, or facts?
5. Check that the recommendation follows from the evidence and the user's priorities.

Deliver the finding, decisive evidence, practical implication, and the next decision or action. Use a short table if several claims need comparison.

If evidence is thin, identify the one additional observation most likely to change the decision. Do not turn repeated copies of one source into independent confirmation or a missing observation into proof of failure.
