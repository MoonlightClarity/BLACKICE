# Map Market Substitution

Use this method when a market is being defined by familiar company names or a product category. Reconstruct the choices available to the customer and the suppliers capable of responding. Treat the boundary as a hypothesis tied to a decision, not a fixed label.

## Primitives

A customer seeks an outcome under budget, time and capability constraints. Alternatives include another product, internal production, a manual workaround, delaying action and doing nothing. Switching may require migration, retraining, coordination or lost compatibility. Suppliers need resources, distribution and time to enter.

## Procedure

1. Specify the customer, task and decision occasion. A small team buying monthly software and a regulated institution signing a multiyear contract can face different substitutes.
2. List feasible alternatives for the same outcome. Include alternatives outside the product category and explain the constraint that makes each feasible or infeasible.
3. Trace total switching cost. Separate one-time migration from recurring operating cost, contractual restrictions and network or data dependencies.
4. Ask what changes if the current offer becomes more expensive, slower or less reliable. Identify which alternatives become attractive and the conditions under which customers can actually adopt them.
5. Inspect supply response. Determine who could provide a substitute, which capabilities they already possess and what would delay entry.
6. Segment the map when constraints differ materially. Record evidence from actual switching, purchase choices, customer tasks or credible experiments; label stated intentions separately.
7. Derive a concrete implication for positioning, product investment or research. Choose the unresolved substitution question that could reverse that implication.

## Worked example

A team sells meeting transcription software. A list of other transcription vendors misses customers who record no transcript, assign a note-taker or replace a meeting with written updates. For a team that needs searchable quotations, manual notes may be a weak substitute. For a team that needs only decisions and owners, structured meeting notes may be sufficient.

If migration requires importing years of recordings, that cost can delay switching even when the alternative has a lower subscription price. The resulting strategy should distinguish customers seeking searchable archives from customers seeking action lists. Interviewing both groups with the same generic preference question would obscure the mechanism.

## Output and check

Produce a customer-task-alternative matrix with switching requirements, evidence and expected response. Check whether each listed competitor solves the specified task and whether excluded alternatives were rejected for an explicit reason.

## Failure boundary

This is an economic and product reasoning method, not a legal market-definition determination. Do not infer market power from share within a conveniently narrow category. Verify current offerings, costs and access conditions when applying the map.
