# Test Adversarial Accounts

Use this method when a source benefits from shaping the audience's belief and may control the evidence being shown. Model what the source can reveal, conceal or fabricate. Keep ordinary error, selection and misunderstanding among the alternatives rather than assuming deception from incentives alone.

## Primitives

Identify the source, intended audience, belief being induced and action that belief would encourage. Evidence has a production process and a cost of fabrication or concealment. Independent constraints may limit the story. A signal that is expensive for one actor can be cheap for another.

## Procedure

1. Extract the factual claims and implied conclusions separately. Identify the behavior the source appears to want from the audience.
2. Map the source's access and control. Determine which records, samples, timings or comparisons the source can select and which observations originate independently.
3. Construct at least one nondeceptive explanation and one strategic-disclosure explanation. Specify what each predicts beyond the published claim.
4. Trace physical, financial, temporal or organizational constraints that the account must satisfy. Prefer independently observed constraints over repeated assertions.
5. Examine omissions in context. Ask whether missing evidence should exist, whether it would be accessible and whether there are ordinary reasons for its absence.
6. Seek an observation that is difficult to produce under the misleading account but expected under the accurate account. Explain that difficulty concretely; do not call a signal impossible to fake without support.
7. Report which claim is supported, contradicted or unresolved. State the implication for the user's decision and the evidence that would revise the judgment.

## Worked example

A vendor demonstrates that its system completes a difficult task in seconds. It controls the chosen inputs, the recording and the displayed timer. The demonstration is compatible with a fast general system, a selected easy case, preprocessing or a narrower capability.

An independently chosen task, a complete timing boundary and an observable output can distinguish these accounts. Failure to provide such a test does not by itself prove fraud, but it leaves the general performance claim unsupported. A purchase decision can therefore be based on the demonstrated scope, with any broader capability treated as an unresolved condition.

## Output and check

Produce a claim-disclosure-constraint map, competing explanations and the result of the most informative available check. Verify that an attributed motive is supported or explicitly hypothetical.

## Failure boundary

Do not equate disagreement, privacy, marketing or missing information with deception. Avoid treating a plausible alternative story as a discovered fact. A source can be biased and still provide accurate evidence.
