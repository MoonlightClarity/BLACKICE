# Compare Competing Accounts

Use this method when several explanations can fit the visible facts or an early account is becoming entrenched. Compare explanations against the same evidence. Seek observations that discriminate between them rather than accumulating facts compatible with the favored account.

## Primitives

A hypothesis predicts observations through a mechanism and auxiliary assumptions. Evidence can support several hypotheses at once. Independent evidence, repeated reporting and absence of an expected observation have different implications. Some explanations overlap or operate together; a table does not make them mutually exclusive.

## Procedure

1. State the phenomenon and the exact question. Separate explaining what happened from predicting what happens next.
2. Construct a small set of materially different explanations, including a mundane process or measurement explanation where plausible. Keep combinations when mechanisms can coexist.
3. List relevant observations with provenance, timing and reliability. Collapse copied reports into their originating evidence for independence judgments.
4. For each observation, ask how expected it would be under each explanation and which auxiliary assumptions are required. Highlight incompatibilities and genuinely discriminating evidence.
5. Identify missing observations that would be expected if an explanation were true. Use absence only after checking whether the observation would have been detectable and recorded.
6. Inspect sensitivity to weak evidence and contested assumptions. Remove or reverse them temporarily and see whether the ranking changes.
7. State the best-supported account, unresolved alternatives and the next discriminating observation. Use numerical probabilities only when their meaning and basis can be justified.

## Worked example

A supplier misses several shipments. Candidate accounts are a production bottleneck, a financing shortage and incorrect order processing. A delayed shipment is compatible with all three. A public statement about strong demand is weakly diagnostic.

Completed goods waiting at the factory would weaken a production-shortage account while leaving financing or shipping administration plausible. Verified unpaid freight bills would discriminate further. If those bills are merely reported by several outlets copying one anonymous source, treat them as one uncertain observation. The output should explain what the evidence changes, not declare whichever column contains the most favorable marks the winner.

## Output and check

Return a compact evidence-by-account table, the discriminating observations, sensitivity result and current conclusion. Verify that rival accounts were represented fairly and that shared assumptions are visible.

## Failure boundary

An ordinal consistency score is not a calibrated probability, and counting contradictions can overvalue several dependent observations. The hypothesis set may be incomplete. Preserve unresolved combinations when available evidence does not separate them.
