# Teach Through Conceptual Distinctions

Teach a concept by helping a learner distinguish valid examples from near misses, explain the underlying rule, and transfer it to a new problem. Check understanding through a fresh application.

## Primitives

A concept groups cases by a relevant property or relationship. Surface similarity can differ from structural similarity. A procedure is justified by conditions that make its steps valid. A learner's incorrect answer can arise from missing knowledge, a mistaken distinction or an execution error.

## Procedure

1. State what the learner should be able to recognize, explain or do after the instruction. Use an observable task rather than understand the topic as the endpoint.
2. Identify prerequisite ideas and the decisive distinction. When a learner response is available, use it to locate the misconception; otherwise make the assumed starting knowledge explicit.
3. Create a minimal worked example. Explain why each step follows and which condition permits it, keeping incidental complexity low.
4. Pair it with a near-miss case that changes the decisive condition while retaining similar surface features. Also include a differently worded case with the same underlying structure.
5. Ask for or provide a transfer exercise whose answer is not a copied substitution. In a requested finished artifact, include the exercise and answer rationale without waiting for a live response.
6. Diagnose errors by the step or distinction they reveal. Change the explanation accordingly instead of repeating it more slowly.
7. Reduce support as competence appears. Preserve enough feedback for the learner to distinguish a correct reason from a lucky answer.

## Worked example

Teach the difference between a percentage increase and an absolute increase. An amount of 100 increasing by 10% becomes 110. Another 10% increase uses the new base and gives 121. Adding 10 twice gives 120 and represents a different rule.

A near-miss example explicitly adds 10 units each period. A transfer task starts at 80 and asks for a 25% increase followed by a 20% decrease. The result is 80 because the second percentage uses 100 as its base. The explanation should identify the changing base, not teach that opposite-sounding percentages generally cancel.

## Output and check

Produce the target performance, decisive distinction, worked example, contrasting case and transfer task with rationale. Check that the transfer task requires the intended concept and does not depend on an untaught trick.

## Failure boundary

A correct worked example does not establish learning. A single correct answer does not establish robust transfer. Avoid increasing difficulty by adding irrelevant reading or arithmetic demands when the target is a conceptual distinction.
