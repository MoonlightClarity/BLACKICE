# Construct Negotiation Trades

Use this method to prepare an agreement that serves the user's objective. Identify what each party can do without an agreement and which terms they value differently. Turn that structure into concrete packages instead of relying on a single demand, a guessed fair midpoint or an invented claim about leverage.

## Primitives

Each party has feasible alternatives, constraints and preferences across terms. An agreement is useful only relative to those alternatives. Price is one issue among timing, scope, certainty, support, flexibility and risk allocation. A stated position may conceal several different underlying interests.

## Procedure

1. State the user's desired result and actual constraints. Distinguish requirements from initial preferences and identify who has authority to agree.
2. Specify the user's best feasible alternative and its costs, timing and uncertainty. Do not describe an unavailable option as bargaining power.
3. Identify the other party's plausible alternatives and interests from evidence. Keep uncertain inferences separate from known terms.
4. List negotiable dimensions and rank their importance to each party. Look for differences in preferences that allow a low-cost concession to provide high value.
5. Construct two or three internally consistent packages. Compare each with the user's alternative, including hidden obligations and downstream consequences.
6. Identify the question or bounded offer that would reveal the most decision-relevant information. Prepare concessions as conditional exchanges rather than unrelated giveaways.
7. Define the terms required for an executable agreement. Record unresolved items and the actual authority needed to finalize them; do not claim acceptance before it is observed.

## Worked example

A buyer values monthly flexibility and needs reliable support. A vendor values predictable annual revenue and low support variability. A 10% price discount alone may be unattractive to the vendor.

Construct one monthly package at the existing price with standard support and another annual package with a lower price, defined support hours and a documented exit condition for persistent service failure. Compare the annual commitment's cost with the buyer's credible alternative. If the buyer expects only three months of use, the discounted annual contract can cost more in total. The relevant tradeoff is the full package, not the discount percentage.

## Output and check

Return the alternatives, issue priorities, package terms, next question or offer, and conditions for agreement. Check that every claimed alternative is feasible and that each concession has a purpose.

## Failure boundary

Do not invent competing offers, authority or facts about the counterpart. A bargaining model does not reveal private preferences automatically. Keep preparation separate from sending offers or accepting terms unless those actions are authorized.
