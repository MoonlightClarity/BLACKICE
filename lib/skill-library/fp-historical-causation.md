# Reconstruct Historical Causes

Use this method to explain a past decision or event without projecting later knowledge into the participants' situation. Reconstruct sequence, available alternatives and plausible mechanisms. Use comparison to test the explanation's boundaries, while preserving the limits of incomplete records.

## Primitives

An event has a time, participants, preceding conditions and observable consequences. A document records something produced by a particular person for a particular audience and purpose. Contemporary belief, actual conditions and later interpretation are different evidence types. Chronological precedence is necessary for a proposed cause but does not establish causation.

## Procedure

1. Define the event and the outcome to explain. Choose a time window wide enough to include relevant preceding conditions without treating every earlier event as a cause.
2. Build a dated timeline from sources. Separate event dates, document creation dates and later publication dates; record uncertainty where ordering matters.
3. Reconstruct what participants could reasonably know and do at each decision point. Distinguish information available somewhere from information demonstrably available to the actor.
4. Propose causal mechanisms linking conditions to actions and outcomes. Compare structural constraints, immediate triggers, individual choices and chance where supported.
5. Inspect source production and survival. Ask whose records remain, whose are missing and whether later accounts had reasons to reinterpret events.
6. Compare relevant cases by causal conditions rather than superficial resemblance. Use a similar outcome with different conditions, or similar conditions with a different outcome, to test the proposed mechanism.
7. State the supported explanation and remaining alternatives. Make counterfactual claims conditional on the specific change and the other conditions assumed to remain.

## Worked example

In a hypothetical town, a factory closes in June after a transport tariff takes effect in April. A later memoir blames the tariff. Contemporary board minutes from January record an already approved closure because equipment replacement was unaffordable.

The tariff may have affected the final months, but it cannot explain the January decision without additional evidence. A nearby factory that remained open is informative only after comparing equipment, financing and transport dependence. Its survival alone does not establish that the tariff had no effect.

## Output and check

Return a timeline, source-context notes, causal mechanisms, comparative observations and a bounded conclusion. Check every proposed causal arrow against temporal order and the actor's information at that time.

## Failure boundary

Do not mistake an available document for a representative account, a later recollection for contemporary knowledge, or a two-case comparison for an identified causal effect. Preserve disagreement when the surviving record cannot resolve it.
