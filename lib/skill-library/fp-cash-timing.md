# Reconcile Cash Timing

Use this method when reported growth or profit conflicts with the cash available to operate. Model cash as a dated stock with explicit inflows and outflows. Preserve the difference between earning revenue, recording a cost, issuing an invoice and settling money.

## Primitives

Opening cash plus receipts minus payments equals closing cash for the chosen period. Receivables are claims to future collections; inventory ties up resources before a sale; payables defer payments. Financing changes available cash without establishing operating profitability. Accounting recognition and cash settlement may occur on different dates.

## Procedure

1. Fix the period and account boundary. Identify restricted balances, currencies and transfers between included accounts so internal movement is not counted as income.
2. Start from verified opening cash. List actual receipts and payments by settlement date, retaining uncertain dates as ranges.
3. Reconcile operating profit to operating cash using relevant noncash items and working-capital changes. Explain the direction of each adjustment rather than memorizing signs.
4. Add investing and financing flows separately. Keep asset purchases, loan proceeds, principal repayment and owner contributions visible.
5. Extend the schedule with explicit collection and payment assumptions. Tie incoming cash to invoice cohorts and terms; tie outflows to obligations and planned commitments.
6. Test the timing mismatch. Move a large collection later, increase inventory needs, or apply a realistic payment delay. Find the lowest cash balance, not merely the ending balance.
7. Identify the action that addresses the mechanism: faster collection, altered payment terms, smaller inventory commitments, lower spending or financing. State its operational consequence and the evidence required to rely on it.

## Worked example

A company begins January with 50,000 cash. It delivers and invoices 80,000 of work, with customers expected to pay in March. January cash costs are 35,000. It also buys equipment for 12,000 and receives a 20,000 loan.

January closing cash is 50,000 - 35,000 - 12,000 + 20,000 = 23,000. The invoice does not add January cash. If February requires another 30,000 before any collection, the projected balance reaches negative 7,000 even though the January sale may be profitable. A quarterly ending balance can hide this intervening shortage. The useful next step is a dated collection schedule and a way to bridge the February gap.

## Output and check

Produce a dated cash bridge with opening balance, operating, investing and financing movements, closing balance and minimum projected balance. Reconcile balances to statements and ensure each cash movement appears once.

## Failure boundary

Do not infer collection certainty from a signed contract or financing availability from an intention to apply. Do not label all depreciation or working-capital adjustments the same across different accounting contexts. Use current records for the actual organization.
