# Derive Needs from Qualitative Evidence

Use this method to turn interviews, observations and task sessions into supported explanations and design decisions. Preserve the route from evidence to interpretation. Examine variation and contradictory cases rather than treating memorable quotations or repeated words as established population facts.

## Primitives

An observation records what occurred in a particular context. A participant's statement records their account, preference or interpretation. A researcher inference connects evidence to a possible mechanism. A proposed solution is a further step and may not follow uniquely from the identified need.

## Procedure

1. State the research question, participant selection and context. Explain which experiences the sample includes and which it does not.
2. Organize raw observations with enough provenance to return to the source: participant or session identifier, task, time and relevant surrounding context.
3. Separate observed behavior, reported behavior, interpretation and proposed change. Preserve quotations accurately when used and avoid making a paraphrase appear verbatim.
4. Group evidence by recurring mechanisms or meaningful distinctions. Define each theme so another reader can understand why an observation belongs there.
5. Compare cases within and across groups. Seek examples that contradict the theme, reveal a different mechanism or show that the same behavior serves different needs.
6. Translate supported mechanisms into need statements and candidate interventions. Trace each intervention back to evidence and identify the assumption that still requires testing.
7. Choose a focused next action: improve a flow, test a prototype, inspect records or recruit a missing context. Explain what that action would resolve.

## Worked example

Five participants struggle to find a saved document. Three search using a client name, one uses a date and one expects it in a project folder. A simple theme of poor search hides different retrieval strategies.

Record the query, expected location and actual result for each case. The evidence suggests testing client metadata and a project route, while preserving date-based retrieval. It does not establish that 60% of the entire user population searches by client. A follow-up task can test whether the proposed metadata helps participants find a document they actually need.

## Output and check

Return an evidence-to-theme-to-need table, contradictory cases, sample limits and the proposed intervention or next observation. Check that every conclusion can be traced to evidence and that interpretation is distinguishable from observation.

## Failure boundary

Frequency in a purposive sample is not automatically a population prevalence estimate. Agreement among interviewees may reflect the prompt or shared context. Do not erase a severe but uncommon failure merely because its theme has fewer mentions.
