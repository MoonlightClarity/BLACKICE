# Compare Making, Buying and Contracting

Use this method when an organization must decide where an activity should be performed. Compare the full arrangement required to deliver the outcome over time. The visible purchase price and internal implementation estimate are only parts of the problem.

## Primitives

A transaction includes specification, production, coordination, monitoring, adaptation and resolution of failures. Some investments are valuable mainly within one relationship. Uncertain future requirements create adaptation needs. Internal provision has management and opportunity costs; external provision has contracting and dependency costs.

## Procedure

1. Define the required outcome, quality, volume, response time and expected duration. Use the same acceptance conditions for every option.
2. Construct realistic alternatives: build internally, buy a standard product, customize a vendor offering, contract for service, or combine these at a stable interface.
3. Estimate lifecycle resources for each: setup, integration, operation, support, adaptation, monitoring, failure recovery and exit. Include the opportunity cost of scarce internal staff.
4. Identify relationship-specific investments and who controls them. Inspect data portability, interfaces, documentation, specialized equipment and the ability to replace a participant.
5. Explore plausible changes in demand, regulation, technical requirements and supplier behavior. Explain who can adapt the arrangement, how quickly and at what cost.
6. Compare options under these scenarios. Avoid adding arbitrary risk scores to currency amounts; show money, time and capability constraints in compatible units or separately.
7. Choose the option or reversible pilot that best meets the stated objective. Record the condition that would make switching arrangements worthwhile.

## Worked example

A team can build an integration for 60 engineer-days or buy a service for 12,000 per year. The purchase also requires 15 engineer-days of integration, five days per quarter of adaptation and an estimated 20-day exit migration. An internal version requires 60 initial days and ten maintenance days per quarter.

Over two years, the external option uses 55 engineering days before exit and 75 including exit; the internal option uses 140. If a required capability is absent from the vendor and cannot be contracted, the cheaper engineering total does not make the external option feasible. Conversely, ownership alone does not justify building if the team cannot maintain the system.

## Output and check

Return comparable acceptance conditions, a lifecycle ledger, adaptation and exit analysis, and a recommendation with reversal conditions. Verify that both alternatives include maintenance and that internal labor is not treated as free.

## Failure boundary

Transaction-cost reasoning is a model of organizational arrangements, not a rule that one ownership form always wins. Use actual contract terms and current capabilities; do not infer enforceable rights from sales language.
