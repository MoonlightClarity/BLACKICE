# Derive Policy Mechanisms and Delivery

Use this method when a policy, program or organizational intervention is expected to produce a desirable outcome. Expand the proposed mechanism and the practical delivery chain. Identify whether the weakest link concerns the theory, the implementation or the evidence used to judge success.

## Primitives

Resources enable activities; activities create exposures or outputs; participants respond under their own constraints; those responses may change outcomes. A decision to implement is not implementation. The counterfactual is what would occur without the intervention, not necessarily a world with no activity.

## Procedure

1. State the intended outcome, affected population, time horizon and comparison condition. Distinguish the policy's objective from a convenient delivery metric.
2. Expand the causal chain into explicit links. For each link, name the actor, action, required condition and observable change.
3. Map delivery dependencies: authority, funding, staff, information, infrastructure, incentives and ownership. Identify what already exists and what the intervention must create.
4. Examine participant responses, substitution, displacement and spillovers. Include ways the intervention could move the activity rather than improve its total effect.
5. Assign evidence to the links. Separate a plausible mechanism, demonstrated delivery, observed intermediate response and identified outcome effect.
6. Choose an evaluation or pilot that resolves the most decision-relevant uncertainty. Measure both implementation and outcomes so a failed result can be interpreted.
7. Revise the intervention based on the observed failure point. A delivery failure and a failed causal theory require different changes.

## Worked example

A program funds training to improve employment. The mechanism requires eligible participants to hear about it, enroll, attend, acquire a useful skill, demonstrate it to employers and obtain available work.

Counting completed courses establishes only one intermediate result. If attendance is low because the schedule conflicts with paid work, changing curriculum may miss the delivery constraint. If graduates acquire the skill but employers cannot recognize it, test a credible work sample or employer connection. Compare employment outcomes with a justified counterfactual and examine whether placements displace other applicants rather than increasing total opportunities.

## Output and check

Return a causal-and-delivery chain, evidence for each link, the weakest unresolved condition and a concrete intervention or evaluation step. Check that resources have owners, dependencies are executable and the outcome measure extends beyond activity completion.

## Failure boundary

A diagram is not causal evidence. Before-after improvement can reflect other changes, and a successful pilot may depend on support absent at scale. Keep implementation assumptions and causal identification assumptions distinct.
