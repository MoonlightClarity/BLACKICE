# Job searching

Use this skill for finding, screening or applying to jobs. Also use blackice-browser for navigation and recovery. A request to find jobs is not permission to submit applications; follow the user's actual authorized scope without adding routine approval rituals.

## Recover the search brief
Use the user's latest role, location/remote eligibility, pay, employment type, travel and other constraints. Read relevant profile/resume and prior outcomes once, then reuse them. Check prior submissions by employer, title and requisition ID before applying; similar titles alone neither prove nor disprove a duplicate. Do not restart completed applications.

## Search where the listings live
For a broad search, start with relevant job boards and their native job search, not a generic Google query. Select a general, specialist or regional board appropriate to the work; examples include LinkedIn Jobs, Indeed and domain-specific boards. Do not assume every board is accessible or equally useful. Use native keywords/title variants, location, remote, pay, date and employment-type filters that the site actually offers. Remote is not necessarily worldwide; verify geographic eligibility and posting details. Do not silently tighten user constraints or treat missing optional filter metadata as automatic rejection.

For a named employer or known opening, go directly to its careers page or observed ATS link. Use board results to discover roles, then prefer the employer's current posting and application route for authoritative requirements and submission. Check posting dates, requisition identity and whether applications remain open. General web search is a supplement for finding the official route, uncovered employers, niche listings or recovering inaccessible board searches, not a ban and not the sole default source.

## Screen efficiently
Read the actual description, not only a search snippet. Separate explicit must-haves from preferences and unknowns; weigh fit against the user's constraints and reasonable transferable experience. Never invent tenure, qualifications, authorization facts or employer answers. Keep a compact shortlist with employer, role, canonical URL/requisition, material fit evidence and status. Avoid exhaustive low-value research on an obviously unsuitable role. If a source yields stale, duplicate or irrelevant results, change keywords, filters or source rather than repeating identical searches.

## Apply and reconcile
For authorized applications, use the employer/ATS route when practical, reuse the appropriate verified resume and profile, and inspect the live form before filling. If a board/social-login route is blocked, look for an observed employer-direct route or supported email login instead of abandoning the role. Do not invent an account, mailbox access or a submission. Preserve drafts and completed steps across interruption.

After submit, inspect the confirmation page, application record or employer acknowledgment. Record confirmed, uncertain, draft, rejected or not submitted distinctly with supporting evidence; a click or thank-you-like label alone may be insufficient to identify which application was received. Reconcile uncertainty before resubmitting. Continue with the next suitable role within the user's time/count limits, preserving deadlines and stop instructions. Report verified submissions and remaining work without equating browsing volume with progress.
